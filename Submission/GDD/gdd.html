Sprint or Shift

A first-person maze runner where you cannot stop, and the maze changes behind your back.

1. Overview

Elevator pitch. You are in a dark neon maze. If you stand still for two seconds, you are dragged back to the start. Every time you turn your back on a purple wall, the maze rearranges itself. You can't stop and you can't go back. You can only keep moving until you reach the glowing portal.

Design question the prototype tests

Can two simple restrictions, "don't stop" and "don't look back", turn an ordinary maze into a tense experience where players must commit to their choices?

Design pillars

PillarWhat it means for design decisions
Momentum over cautionEvery rule rewards moving and punishes hesitating. There is no pause, no map and no slow "careful" option.
The maze is watchingThe world reacts to where you look. Walls change only behind your back, so looking (or not looking) is part of how you play.
Readable rules, chaotic resultsEach rule is simple and stated on a wall sign. The chaos comes from how the rules combine, not from randomness.
Small and focusedTwo levels that test the idea well, instead of a large game that isn't finished.

Player experience goals

  • Urgency: the player's heart rate goes up the moment they stop.
  • Paranoia: players think twice before turning around.
  • Mastery: players discover that standing still is also a deliberate way out of a dead end.

2. Core rules & controls

1
Hesitation penalty: "DON'T STOP". If your horizontal speed stays below 0.1 m/s for 2.0 s, you are teleported to the START point. A red overlay with a countdown fades in from 0.3 s to warn you. The timer only starts after your first step, so waiting at START is safe.
2
Wall shift: "DON'T LOOK BACK". Purple walls change only after you have seen them (on screen with a clear line of sight) and they are now behind you (more than 110° from where you're looking). One-way gates close after you pass through them. Toggle walls flip open or closed each time.
3
Dead ends & reset. Dead ends are deliberate traps. If you're boxed in, standing still for 2 s sends you back to START, and every purple wall resets to its starting state, so the level is always solvable.
★
Win condition. Touch the glowing cyan Finish Portal. Level 1 leads to Level 2, which leads to the Victory screen (press R to play again).

3. Core loop

Second to second, the player runs and chooses at junctions under the 2-second pressure. Minute to minute, they learn the maze across resets: each teleport costs time but teaches them the layout and where the traps are. Across the game, Level 1 introduces each rule on its own, and Level 2 combines them all with faster walls.

4. Mechanics in detail

4.1 Wall-shift state machine

Wall typeStartsShifts when…Purpose
One-way gate (cyan arrow on the maps)OpenSeen, the player has walked ≥ 3 m past it in the arrow direction, and it is now behind them. Shifts once only.Stops the player retracing their steps and turns wrong choices into dead ends.
Toggle wall (⇄ on the maps)OpenSeen, then behind the player. Flips every time. Cooldown is 2.0 s in L1 and 0.6 s in L2.Changes the paths: creates shortcuts or seals off a region.

Safety rules: a wall never shifts while the player is within 1.5 m of it, so it can't trap them inside the wall. Every wall resets when the player is teleported.

4.2 Stillness rule tuning

ParameterValueWhy
Stillness limit2.0 sLong enough to turn a corner and read a sign. Too short to stop and study the maze.
Speed threshold0.1 m/sUses the speed the player actually moves at, so pushing into a wall counts as standing still. This is what makes Rule 3 work.
Warning overlayFades in from 15%–60% of the limitIgnores tiny pauses, then makes the danger obvious.

5. Level design

Both levels use 4 m grid cells. They are written as text layouts in SprintOrShiftLevelBuilder.cs, so designers can edit a maze by typing characters. Both layouts were checked with a breadth-first-search solver: the exit can be reached even when every toggle wall is closed.

Level 1: Tutorial ("DON'T STOP. DON'T LOOK BACK.")

Teaching beats: (1) the first thing the player sees is "DON'T STOP". (2) "DON'T LOOK BACK" is placed just before the first gate, which closes behind them a few seconds later. (3) "KEEP MOVING" is placed before the second gate and the portal.

Level 2: High Chaos

  • Trick corridor (sign 3, "DEAD END"): leads towards the centre and stops exactly one wall short of the real path (gate ^ next to it). This teaches players not to trust where a corridor seems to lead.
  • Shortcut toggle (next to sign 5, "TRUST NOTHING"): an open shortcut towards the exit that closes once it's behind you. If you leave it, you have to take the long way round.
  • Left region: a tempting open area full of dead ends. It's reachable through a toggle wall that can close behind you.

6. Visual style & feedback

Dark neon toon. The world is built only from Unity primitive cubes, with flat colours and strong contrast: indigo walls, a near-black floor and dark fog. Anything important glows (bloom): the green START pad, the yellow, red and cyan signs, and the cyan portal. Purple marks the dynamic walls, so attentive players can spot them. A camera flashlight limits how far you can see, which adds to the tension.

7. MDA analysis

Target aesthetics: Sensation and Challenge, with Fantasy (being hunted by a living maze) as flavour. The table follows three connected chains, each running from a mechanic to a dynamic to an aesthetic.

#MechanicDynamicAesthetic
1Stillness timer: speed < 0.1 for 2 s means teleport to START. Countdown overlay.Players never pause. They make split-second choices at junctions and "wiggle" to buy time, which still costs them.Urgency, adrenaline (Sensation, Challenge)
2Line-of-sight wall shifting: a wall is armed when seen and shifts once it's more than 110° behind you.Players avoid turning round, glance back nervously, and stare at walls to "freeze" them.Paranoia, dread (Sensation, Fantasy)
3Dead ends + one-way gates + stillness reset (the reset also restores every wall).Being trapped becomes a choice: players stand still on purpose to escape.Tension, then relief (Challenge, Discovery)

How the chains connect

Chain 1 feeds chain 2: you can't stop to watch your back, so the time pressure is what makes "don't look back" dangerous. Chain 2 feeds chain 3: walls closing behind you create real dead ends. Chain 3 loops back to chain 1: the way out of a trap is the stillness rule itself. One mechanic both punishes and rescues, and players learn to use it both ways.

The Dynamics column describes the intended behaviour. It will be confirmed by the external playtest (Section 10). Chain 2 has already been revised once after the internal playtest showed that walls felt random rather than tense.

8. Design principles applied

PrincipleWhere it appears
Teach through level designLevel 1 is linear, and each sign sits right before the mechanic it describes. Level 2 only combines rules the player already knows.
Clear feedbackThe stillness overlay has a visible countdown. Teleports always show a message. Every important object glows.
Signifiers / affordancesDynamic walls are a different colour (purple) from static walls (indigo). The portal glows, which says "go here".
Difficulty curveLevel 1 has 2 gates and no branches. Level 2 has 5 gates, 2 toggle walls with a 3× faster cooldown, 4 dead ends and a trick corridor.
Meaningful choice / risk–rewardThe toggle shortcut in Level 2 saves time but can close behind you.
Fairness & predictabilityWalls follow one rule (seen, then behind you) and reset on teleport. The exit is always reachable, which was proven by a solver.

9. Technical implementation

Unity 6 (6000.4.0f1), Universal Render Pipeline, the new Input System and TextMeshPro. There are three runtime scripts with one-way dependencies: the player and the walls talk to GameManager.Instance.

ScriptResponsibility
FirstPersonControllerCharacterController movement with smoothed acceleration, smoothed mouse look with pitch clamp, the 2 s stillness timer, TeleportToStart(), and the Finish trigger (built-in Finish tag).
DynamicMazeWallEach frame: frustum test + Physics.Linecast line-of-sight check, a "behind" angle test, cooldown and distance guards, then turns the Renderer and Collider on or off together. ResetWall() restores the starting state.
GameManagerStart Point reference, stillness overlay, centre messages, victory panel, wall reset on teleport, and level transitions (nextSceneName, empty = victory).
SprintOrShiftLevelBuilder (editor only)Builds both scenes from text layouts: walls, gates, toggles, signs, START, portal, player, UI and lighting.

Why not OnBecameInvisible()? In the Editor it also counts the Scene-view camera, it stops firing once a wall hides its own Renderer, and it ignores what is in the way. Testing the player camera directly is the same idea, but predictable.

Hierarchy (identical in both levels): Directional Light, Global Volume, GameManager, Environment (Floor, StartPoint), Maze (Walls / DynamicWalls / Signs), Player → PlayerCamera → Flashlight, UI (Crosshair, StillnessOverlay, MessageText, VictoryPanel), FinishPortal.

10. Playtesting & iteration

Round 1: internal playtest (team, in the Editor)

ObservationChange
"The pink boxes for the dead ends are always there."Debug gizmos were drawn in the Game view. They now draw only when a wall is selected. Fixed
"Moving the mouse is really slow."Sensitivity raised from 0.12 to 0.45 and smoothing from 20 to 30. Fixed
"The purple walls seem random. Sometimes they're up before I've looked back."Added line of sight and a "behind you" angle, and all walls now start open. Fixed. This was the most important change: it moved chain 2 from confusion to paranoia.

Round 2: automated rule checks

Scripted play-mode tests passed for: stillness teleport, gate closes behind the player, walls reset on teleport, walls hidden behind other walls don't count as seen, turning 90° to the side does not trigger a shift, turning fully round does, and the portal loads Level 2. A solver confirmed both mazes can be completed.

Round 3: external playtest

Not yet conducted. Plan: 3 to 5 new players, controls only, about 10 minutes each. Tally junction pauses, turn-arounds and intentional resets (one per MDA chain), then a 5-question interview. The full script and note sheet are in the Playtesting Notes document.
